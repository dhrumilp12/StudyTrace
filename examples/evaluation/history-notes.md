# French Revolution: chronology
The Estates-General met at Versailles in May 1789 after a long royal financial crisis.
The National Assembly adopted the Tennis Court Oath on 20 June 1789.
A Paris crowd stormed the Bastille on 14 July 1789.
The National Assembly abolished feudal privileges on 4 August 1789.
# Rights and institutions
The Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen was adopted in August 1789.
The declaration stated that sovereignty resides in the nation rather than the monarch.
The Constitution of 1791 established a constitutional monarchy with a legislative assembly.
Voting rights under the 1791 constitution were limited rather than universal.
# Republic and war
France abolished the monarchy and proclaimed a republic in September 1792.
Louis XVI was executed in January 1793 after being tried by the National Convention.
The Committee of Public Safety coordinated war policy and emergency government during the Terror.
The fall of Robespierre in July 1794 ended the most intense phase of the Terror.
# Limits and aftermath
Olympe de Gouges challenged the exclusion of women from full political citizenship in 1791.
The Directory governed France from 1795 until Napoleon's coup in November 1799.
Napoleon's coup replaced the Directory with the Consulate and concentrated executive power.
These dates describe different institutional changes and should not be treated as one event.
