# Describing data
The mean is the sum of all observations divided by the number of observations.
The median is the middle ordered value, or the mean of the two middle values for an even sample.
An extreme outlier can strongly affect the mean while leaving the median almost unchanged.
The sample standard deviation describes the spread of individual observations around their mean.
# Sampling and uncertainty
The standard error of the mean describes the variability of sample means across repeated samples.
For independent observations with finite variance, increasing sample size generally decreases the standard error of the mean.
SE = s / sqrt(n)
A 95% confidence procedure covers the fixed population parameter in 95% of repeated samples under its assumptions.
A realized confidence interval does not assign a 95% probability to a fixed frequentist parameter.
# Probability
Conditional probability restricts the sample space to the event that is known to have occurred.
P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B)
P(B) > 0
Independence means that learning event B occurred does not change the probability of event A.
P(A and B) = P(A) * P(B)
# Interpretation
Correlation measures association and does not by itself establish causation.
A small p-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true.
Statistical significance is not the same as a large or practically important effect.
A randomized experiment can support causal conclusions when its design and analysis assumptions are justified.
