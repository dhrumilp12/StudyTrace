# Acids and equilibrium
A Brønsted acid donates a proton, whereas a Brønsted base accepts a proton.
A strong acid dissociates nearly completely in dilute water, but a weak acid establishes an equilibrium.
The acid dissociation constant Ka measures the equilibrium tendency of an acid to donate a proton in water.
pH = -log10[H+]
pKa = -log10(Ka)
At 25 degrees Celsius, neutral pure water has pH 7.00.
# Buffers
A buffer contains a weak acid and its conjugate base or a weak base and its conjugate acid.
A buffer resists a large pH change when a small amount of acid or base is added.
The Henderson-Hasselbalch equation relates pH, pKa, and the ratio of conjugate base to acid concentrations.
pH = pKa + log10([A-] / [HA])
A buffer has limited capacity and can be overwhelmed by adding too much strong acid or strong base.
# Reaction rates
A catalyst increases reaction rate by providing a pathway with lower activation energy.
A catalyst does not change the equilibrium constant or the final equilibrium composition.
Increasing temperature usually increases reaction rate because a greater fraction of collisions can overcome the activation barrier.
Rate laws are determined experimentally and cannot generally be read directly from an overall reaction equation.
# Stoichiometry
The limiting reactant is the reactant that is consumed first according to the balanced reaction proportions.
Theoretical yield is calculated from the amount of limiting reactant and the balanced reaction equation.
Percent yield compares the actual product amount with the theoretical product amount in the same units.
percent yield = 100 * actual / theoretical
